Title: Where Ideas Come From (sample)
URL: synthetic://pg-essays/sample-on-ideas

NOTE: This is a SYNTHETIC sample essay used only to demo the walking skeleton.
It is not a real Paul Graham essay. Run `python tools/crawl.py` to fetch the
real corpus.

The best ideas don't feel like ideas when they arrive. They feel like problems —
specifically, problems you keep running into and keep working around. The working
around is the tell. Whenever you build a little hack to avoid an annoyance, you've
found the edge of something missing in the world.

People look for ideas by sitting still and thinking hard. That rarely works,
because ideas are downstream of experience. You don't find them by introspection;
you find them by doing enough real things that the gaps become visible. Live in
the future, then build what's missing — the future being just the set of problems
that the people closest to a domain already feel but haven't named.

The cruelest trap is the idea that sounds good at a dinner party. Those are
optimized for telling, not for building. The ideas worth pursuing usually sound a
little dubious out loud, because if they were obviously good someone would already
have done them.
