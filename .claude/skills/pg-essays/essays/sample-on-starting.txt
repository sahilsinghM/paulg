Title: On Starting Things (sample)
URL: synthetic://pg-essays/sample-on-starting

The hardest part of starting something is not the idea but the deciding. Most
people wait for permission that never comes. They imagine that one day they will
feel ready, and that readiness will arrive like weather. It won't. You start, and
the readiness assembles itself behind you out of the things you learn by starting.

A good first project is small enough to finish and real enough to embarrass you a
little. If it doesn't embarrass you, you aimed too low. The embarrassment is a
sign that you reached past what you already knew how to do, which is the only
direction worth reaching.

Do not ask whether the thing is impressive. Ask whether it is alive — whether it
does something, for someone, today. Impressive is a judgment other people make
later. Alive is a property you can check yourself, right now, by using the thing.
